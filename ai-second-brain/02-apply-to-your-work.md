# How to apply this to your own work

The video uses content creation as its example, but the method works for any job:

> **Context (second brain) + your process written as skills + small agents with sharp jobs + data + you as the final judge.**

Work through the steps below in order. Each one takes 30–60 minutes, and the worksheets are meant to be filled in.

---

## Step 1: Split your work into "AI does it" and "I decide it"

Vaibhav's rule: AI does the low-value operational ~80%, and you own the last ~20%.

| Give to AI (operational) | Keep for yourself (judgment) |
|---|---|
| Collecting information and research | Choosing *which* idea, client or project to pursue |
| Summarising, transcribing, tagging | Deciding *why* and setting priorities |
| First drafts and variations (10–100 options) | Picking the final 1–2 options |
| Pulling data, writing SQL, building reports | Asking the right questions of the data |
| Scheduling, follow-ups, tracking, reminders | Taste, voice, relationships |
| Checking a draft against a checklist | Signing off on anything high-stakes |

**Worksheet: list 15–20 tasks you did last week**

| Task | How often | Operational or judgment? | Context the AI would need | Automate first? (Y/N) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. find content ideas | daily | operational (collecting) + judgment (picking) | my niche, past winners, sources I trust | Y (collecting only) |
| | | | | |
| | | | | |

**Rule of thumb:** automate tasks that are frequent, repetitive and need gathering information. Never automate the moment where you choose.

---

## Step 2: Map your context sources (your future second brain)

AI gives average answers because it doesn't see what you see. List where *your* context lives:

- [ ] **What I consume for work:** YouTube channels, podcasts, newsletters, X accounts, Reddit, communities
- [ ] **What I discuss:** WhatsApp groups, Slack/Teams, email threads, voice notes
- [ ] **Meetings:** client calls, team standups, sales calls
- [ ] **My raw thinking:** chats with ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, voice brainstorms, notes apps
- [ ] **My past work:** posts, scripts, proposals, decks, code, reports
- [ ] **Results data:** views and engagement, sales/CRM, ad dashboards, revenue sheets
- [ ] **My SOPs:** how I research, write, sell or decide (even if it's only in my head)

Mark each one **Private** (personal AI chats, finances) or **Team-shareable** (meeting notes, SOPs). Vaibhav keeps personal chats private.

---

## Step 3: Reimagine the process with "unlimited interns"

Don't ask "where can AI help in my current process?" Ask:

> "If I had 100 tireless interns who read everything, what would this process look like?"

For each core process, write the new version as stages:

```
Core process: ______________________
Stage 1 (collect):   sources = ______ → one agent per source
Stage 2 (filter):    score each item against ______ (my past results / others' results)
Stage 3 (shortlist): top N → expand into angles/options
Stage 4 (decide):    ME, choosing from 2–5 options
Stage 5 (produce):   skill-based draft → my edit
Stage 6 (measure):   data agent → daily/weekly report → update the skills
```

---

## Step 4: Pick your first 3 automations

Good first picks are **low-risk, daily and information-heavy**:

1. **A daily digest agent** for your niche or industry (Vaibhav's "GeoWatch" demo)
2. **A second-brain feeder**: videos and meetings turned into short "value cards"
3. **A weekly results report**: social, sales or ads data with "what worked and what didn't"

Leave the clever stuff (100-agent scoring, overnight self-improving loops) until these three have run reliably for 2–3 weeks.

## Examples by type of work

### Content creator / personal brand (what the video shows)
- **Second brain:** the videos you watch in your niche, your past scripts and their performance, your voice notes
- **Agents:** one triage agent each for X, Reels/TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and news → one idea inbox
- **Skills:** your hook formulas, script structure per topic bucket, packaging (titles and thumbnails)
- **Data:** a DNA playbook sheet of every post with its metrics and *why it worked*
- **You:** pick the topic and angle from the shortlist, and add personal stories

### Business owner / agency / freelancer
- **Second brain:** client call transcripts, proposals that won or lost, WhatsApp and email threads, your SOPs
- **Agents:** lead research, inbox triage, subscription and expense tracker, competitor monitor, weekly KPI report
- **Skills:** "how we qualify a lead", "how we write a proposal", "how we onboard a client"
- **Data:** a CRM or sheet of deals with outcomes. Ask *"which things we said on calls led to bigger deals?"* (his 44% ROI case)
- **You:** pricing, which clients to take, final proposal sign-off

### Sales / marketing professional
- **Second brain:** call recordings, objections log, winning emails and ads
- **Agents:** account research before every call, ad-performance watcher, competitor ad tracker
- **Skills:** your outreach style, objection-handling playbook, ad-copy structure
- **Data:** ad account + CRM. Ask "why did ROI drop?", then have AI generate 10–15 hypotheses and check each one
- **You:** strategy, budget allocation, the relationship

### Analyst / operations / any job role
- **Second brain:** meeting notes, the documents you rely on, your past reports
- **Agents:** daily report generator, data-quality checker, meeting-prep brief ("what did we discuss last time with X?")
- **Skills:** a "business intelligence" skill (how your business works, what each metric means) and a definitions skill
- **You:** framing the question and verifying anything that will drive a big decision

### Student / job seeker / developer
- **Second brain:** lectures and tutorials you watch, your notes, the projects you've built
- **Agents:** job-post triage for your keywords, a daily "what's new in my field" digest, interview prep from your own notes
- **Skills:** how you write a cover letter, how you explain a project, your coding conventions
- **You:** which roles to target and what to learn next

## Team guardrails (fixing Raj's "my team got dumber" problem)

1. **AI proposes, humans dispose.** AI may generate 100 options, but a human must shortlist and pick, and write one line on *why*.
2. **Original thought comes first.** Write your own take in 3 bullets *before* asking the AI. Then compare.
3. **Use more than one model.** Ask 2–3 different models (or one model with different personas) so ideas don't converge.
4. **Feed context, not just prompts.** Every important AI task must reference the second brain or a skill, not a blank chat.
5. **Measure human vs AI output.** Like Raj's question A/B test, compare and let the data decide where AI helps.
6. **Verify big moves with a human.** Any money, pricing or public decision gets a human check, however confident the AI sounds.

## Your 12-week roadmap

Vaibhav says 12–18 weeks, and most people quit around week 3. Plan for it.

| Weeks | Goal | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work audit (Step 1) + context map (Step 2) + install one agent harness | Worksheet filled in, harness running |
| 2 | Create the second-brain folder + `AGENTS.md`. Start feeding 1–2 sources | 20+ value cards / notes exist |
| 3 | First daily digest agent on a schedule | Digest arrives daily without you doing anything |
| 4–5 | Write your first 3 skills (your real SOPs) | AI drafts are recognisably *your* style |
| 6–7 | Results data agent (Metricool, APIs or a CSV) + DNA playbook sheet | Weekly "what worked" report |
| 8–9 | Scoring: idea → 1–100 score using your playbook | Shortlist matches your gut most of the time |
| 10–11 | Loop engineering on your most important skill (with an anti-cheat examiner) | Skill scores ≥ 9 against your best past work |
| 12 | Review: cut agents nobody reads, set a 70/30 experiment rule | One page: what to keep, kill, add |
